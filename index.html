Web App · Generative AI · Python

Ski Selector

A generative AI tool that recommends the right ski for you and explains exactly why, drawing on curated retailer data and expert perspectives

+

The Problem

As someone who skis a lot, I kept getting asked by friends what ski to buy. I'd give an opinion but realized I was just guessing based on my own experience. The real problem is that ski selection is genuinely complex: it depends on ability level, skiing style, terrain preference, and budget. Existing tools either oversimplify to a few dropdowns or dump a spec sheet on you with no guidance on what actually matters.

What I Built

A web app that walks users through a short intake flow about their ability, goals, terrain, and budget. It uses RAG to pull from a curated knowledge base of retailer reviews and expert ski guides, then uses an LLM to synthesize a recommendation with a plain-English explanation tailored to that specific person. I intentionally made the reasoning more prominent than the recommendation itself, because that's what actually helps someone feel confident in the purchase.

Python Generative AI RAG Web App
Visit findmyskis.org ↗

PM Instinct Behind It

"When you're the workaround people use because the real tool doesn't exist, that's a product gap worth filling. The key insight from talking to people was that they didn't distrust the recommendation, they just didn't know how to evaluate it. Explaining the 'why' in plain language is what actually builds confidence, so I made it the centerpiece rather than an afterthought."

Web App · Routing · Cycling

SF Road Bike Router

A route planning tool built for road cyclists who are tired of being sent up hills and through busy intersections by apps that don't understand how road biking actually works

+

The Problem

Strava and most GPX tools optimize for distance and connectivity, not ride quality. For road biking specifically, that means you regularly get routed up punishing climbs you didn't ask for, through intersections with heavy car traffic, or onto roads with no shoulder. Every time I wanted to explore a new route around SF I'd spend 30+ minutes in Strava manually adjusting, cross-referencing Google Maps, and checking Street View just to sanity-check the road conditions. It was a whole process before I even got on the bike.

What I Built

A web app where you set a start point, target distance, and preferred effort level, and it generates a route optimized for road cycling — lower traffic streets, manageable elevation, and roads that are actually pleasant to ride. It exports directly to GPX so I can load it onto my Garmin without any extra steps. The whole point was to eliminate the 30-minute pre-ride planning ritual and just get out the door.

Python Mapping API GPX Export Web App

PM Instinct Behind It

"The temptation was to build a general cycling tool, but I knew from my own experience that road biking has very specific needs that are different from mountain biking or commuting. Scoping it tightly to one persona in one geography made the product actually useful rather than mediocre for everyone. I also learned a lot about the tradeoffs between routing APIs, elevation data sources, and what 'low traffic' actually means when you try to operationalize it."
